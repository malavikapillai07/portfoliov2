Objective of the Study

The objective was to translate the craft heritage of Eravathody Handloom Society into a contemporary digital experience that communicates its story, showcases its handloom products and weaving traditions, and improves its visibility in the digital space.

Within scope
  • Craft research and fieldwork
  • Content strategy
  • UX and UI design
  • Website design and development using AI-assisted coding
Beyond scope
  • Payment gateway integration
  • Backend system development
  • Complete e-commerce operations

The website's commerce functionality was limited to the product and billing stages, without payment integration or a fully developed backend.

Timeline: 4 weeks

Design Process

Week 01 — Craft Research & Fieldwork

Understanding the weaving traditions, handloom products, artisans, production processes, and heritage of Eravathody Handloom Society through on-site research and conversations with the people involved.

Week 02 — Content Strategy

Defining the website's objectives, information priorities, content structure, storytelling approach, and the way craft traditions, products, and artisan stories would be communicated digitally.

Week 03 — UX & UI Design

Translating the research into a clear website structure, developing the information architecture, establishing the visual direction, and designing the interface and wireframes.

Week 04 — AI-Assisted Development

Using AI-assisted coding to translate the designs into a functioning website, refining the implementation, and bringing the digital experience together.

Understanding the Craft

The opportunity to stay at Thiruvillamala and interact directly with the weaving community helped develop a deeper understanding of the craft, its processes, its people, and its cultural significance.

Speaking directly with the weavers and interviewing the society's secretary provided first-hand context for the website's content hierarchy and design decisions. Spending time at the pit looms illuminated the careful tension of warp and weft, the heritage of the co-operative established in 1941, and the pride each artisan brings to every weave.

From Pitloom to Digital Presence

Traditional handloom weaving is fundamentally tactile and rooted in slow, deliberate physical craftsmanship. Translating this heritage into a digital presence required establishing meaningful bridges between physical weaving knowledge, the human artisans behind each garment, and the digital visitor seeking authentic handloom.

From a UX perspective, this meant structuring the digital journey so visitors can discover the society's unique identity, understand how unbleached cotton and kasavu zari are woven, explore the curated collection with clarity, and navigate detailed product specifications with ease.

Key Findings — Fieldwork & Research Insights

[Research Visual 01: Weaving Technique & Texture]

Tactile Nuance & Yarn Authenticity

Visitors needed to perceive fabric weight, weave density, and pure kasavu borders digitally. This informed high-detail macro imagery and fabric zoom interactions in the product modal.

[Research Visual 02: Artisan Portrait & Story]

Artisan Recognition & Living Heritage

Weavers were historically invisible in conventional retail channels. The website created a dedicated Artisan showcase honoring master weavers by name, age, and years of service.

[Research Visual 03: Cooperative Society Heritage]

Co-operative Transparency & Trust

Direct institutional trust stems from the society's 1941 establishment. Archival timelines and society background provide shoppers confidence in genuine fair-trade craft.

[Research Visual 04: Order & Billing Accessibility]

Streamlined Direct Ordering

Without complex backend gateways, customers needed a clean, transparent order enquiry and phone-verified dispatch flow that works reliably for regional shoppers.

Problem Statement

How might we honor and represent Eravathody's 83-year-old weaving heritage, its artisan community, and its handloom products through a clear, honest, and accessible contemporary digital presence that builds trust and supports direct customer engagement?

Design System

The design system draws directly from the natural materials of the handloom cluster: unbleached organic cotton, dark loom timber, and subtle kasavu gold.

Typography & Hierarchy

Primary Heading & Interface: Plus Jakarta Sans / Inter Tight (Clean geometric clarity).
Technical & Spec Labels: Space Mono (Architectural rhythm and documentation cadence).

Colour Palette
Loom Brown (#3D2314)
Off-White Canvas (#FCFCFB)
Kasavu Gold (#C9A66B)
Raw Cotton (#EAE2D8)
UI Components & Linework

Generous 20px card corner radii, subtle hairline borders (0.08 alpha), and soft tactile shadows reflect the organic warmth of handloom textiles.

Behind the Scenes — AI-Assisted Development
